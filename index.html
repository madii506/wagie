4.1 — Why we pay in $WDAY

New hires ask why payroll is denominated in a Workday stock token rather than, say, money. The short answer is that Workday makes the software that runs employee self-service portals like this one. The long answer is the best corporate revenge story of the 2000s, and every employee should know it.

4.1.1

In June 2003, Oracle launched a hostile bid for PeopleSoft, the HR-software company Dave Duffield had co-founded in 1987. The opening offer was roughly $13 billion. Over the next eighteen months it went down to about $9.4B, down again to about $7.7B, and back up to $9.4B.[2]

4.1.2

PeopleSoft fought back with a poison pill unlike most: a customer assurance program promising customers refunds of two to five times their licence fees if the company were acquired and support was cut. It did not stop the deal.[2]

4.1.3

Oracle closed the acquisition in December 2004 for about $10.3 billion. A month later it laid off 6,000 of PeopleSoft's 11,000 employees.[2] That is more than half of a company that had been famous for how it treated its people.

4.1.4

In March 2005 — three months after losing his company — Duffield and Aneel Bhusri founded Workday. The product launched in November 2006. In December 2008 the company moved its headquarters to Pleasanton, California: PeopleSoft's home town.[1]

4.1.5

Workday IPO'd on 12 October 2012 at $28 a share and closed its first day at $48.69, raising about $637 million and valuing the company near $9.5 billion.[1] Today its software is the timecard, the PTO request and the pay statement for a meaningful slice of the world's office workers. In fiscal 2025 it reported $8.45 billion in revenue and around 20,400 employees.[1]

4.1.6

One more thing every wagie should know. Duffield's largest philanthropy is Maddie's Fund, an animal-welfare foundation named after his miniature schnauzer, Maddie, who died in 1997.[3] In his words:

"Maddie was the lighthouse during the stormy period."

The company that fired six thousand people made him a billionaire twice. The dog got the foundation.

4.2 — Attendance policy

4.2.1

A workday is any Monday–Friday, measured in UTC. A holder may clock in once per workday by calling clockIn() from a wallet holding WAGIE.

4.2.2

Payday is every second Friday. The attendance pool — 100% of the creator fee share collected since the previous payday — is distributed pro-rata by shifts clocked to every holder still employed.

4.2.3

Three consecutive missed workdays constitute job abandonment. The contract terminates the holder's employment; their shifts for the period are forfeited to colleagues who clocked in. Terminated holders may reapply at any time and start again from zero.

4.2.4

There is no PTO. There is no sick leave. There is no manager to ask. There is a button.

4.3 — About the payroll currency

4.3.1

$WDAY is a stock token on Robinhood Chain that tracks Workday, Inc. It is one of the tokenized US equities Robinhood lists on its chain, and it is the asset WAGIE is paired against on Pons v2. We checked the chain's live asset list before choosing it; ADP and Paychex, the other payroll names, are not listed there.[5]

4.3.2

A stock token is not a share. It carries no voting rights and is not issued by Workday. Read Robinhood's own documentation before treating it as anything more than what it is: the unit your attendance is paid in.

Frequently asked questions

What do I actually have to do?
Hold WAGIE. Once per workday (Mon–Fri, UTC), call clockIn() from the wallet that holds it. That is the entire job description. Every second Friday, the attendance pool is paid out to everyone who clocked in, pro-rata by shifts, in $WDAY.
What happens if I miss a day?
One miss resets your streak. Two misses is a warning you will not receive because there is no HR. Three consecutive misses and the contract terminates you. Your shifts for that period are forfeited to the colleagues who clocked in. You can reapply immediately and start again from zero.
Where does the money come from?
Trading fees, and only trading fees. Pons v2 charges 1% on every trade; 70% of that is the creator share, and 100% of the creator share is the attendance pool. There is no treasury, no team allocation and no "marketing wallet". If nobody trades, payday is 0.0000 $WDAY and the pay statement will say so.
Why is payday in $WDAY and not ETH or a stablecoin?
Because it's funnier, and because Workday, Inc. is the company whose software is the real version of this portal. Robinhood Chain lets a token pair against tokenized US stocks, so the payroll company pays the payroll. Read Handbook §4.1 for the origin story.
Is the timecard on this page real?
It runs in your browser only, so you can learn the rules before the on-chain clock opens. It enforces the same rules the contract will. Nothing you do here is written anywhere but your own browser.
Is there a contract address yet?
Not yet. When there is, it appears on the Company tab, in the pinned post on X, and nowhere else. Anything claiming to be WAGIE before that is not.
Can I get promoted?
No.
