    4.1 — Why we pay in $SPY

    New hires ask why payroll is denominated in an S&P 500 token rather than, say, money. The short answer is that every office worker on earth is already being paid in $SPY — through a retirement plan they are not allowed to touch for forty years. We removed the forty years. The long answer is that the fund itself is one of the strangest financial objects ever built, and every employee should know it.

    4.1.1

    SPY launched on 22 January 1993 and first traded on the American Stock Exchange a week later. It was the first exchange-traded fund in the United States. It was designed by two AMEX product people, Nathan Most and Steven Bloom, with State Street doing the plumbing. Initial issue: 150,000 units.[1]

    4.1.2

    "SPDR" stands for Standard & Poor's Depositary Receipts. Everyone says "spider". It is the most traded security in the world, and in February 2024 it became the first ETF to hold $500 billion; by October 2024, $600 billion.[1]

    4.1.3

    Here is the part nobody's HR department mentions. SPY is legally a unit investment trust — an old structure with no portfolio manager and no board. It can't reinvest dividends between payouts and it can't lend out its shares. It also, by law, has to have an expiry date.[1]

    4.1.4

    So in 1993 the lawyers pegged the trust's lifespan to eleven real children, born around the launch, eight of them relatives of people at the exchange. The fund that half the planet's pensions sit in is scheduled to dissolve twenty years after the last of those eleven people dies — or on 22 January 2118, whichever comes first.[1]

    Somewhere there are eleven millennials whose life expectancy is a material fact for a $600 billion fund. None of them, as far as we know, have ever clocked in.
    4.1.5

    In February 2025 Vanguard's VOO overtook SPY as the world's largest ETF; SPY is still the one that trades. On 26 January 2026 the fund was renamed the State Street SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust.[1] The $SPY token on Robinhood Chain tracks it, which means: every second Friday, WAGIE pays you in the entire American stock market, and the payroll department is a spider with an expiry date.

    4.2 — Attendance policy

    4.2.1

    A workday is any Monday–Friday, measured in UTC. A holder may clock in once per workday by calling clockIn() from a wallet holding WAGIE.

    4.2.2

    Payday is every second Friday. The attendance pool — 100% of the creator fee share collected since the previous payday — is distributed pro-rata by shifts clocked to every holder still employed.

    4.2.3

    Three consecutive missed workdays constitute job abandonment. The contract terminates the holder's employment; their shifts for the period are forfeited to colleagues who clocked in. Terminated holders may reapply at any time and start again from zero.

    4.2.4

    There is no PTO. There is no sick leave. There is no manager to ask. There is a button.

    4.3 — About the payroll currency

    4.3.1

    $SPY is a stock token on Robinhood Chain that tracks the SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust. It is one of the tokenized US equities and ETFs Robinhood lists on its chain, and it is the asset WAGIE is paired against on Pons v2. We checked the chain's live asset list before choosing it.[2]

    4.3.2

    A stock token is not a share and not a fund unit. It carries no voting rights and is not issued by State Street. Read Robinhood's own documentation before treating it as anything more than what it is: the unit your attendance is paid in.

    Frequently asked questions

    What do I actually have to do?
    Hold WAGIE. Once per workday (Mon–Fri, UTC), call clockIn() from the wallet that holds it. That is the entire job description. Every second Friday, the attendance pool is paid out to everyone who clocked in, pro-rata by shifts, in $SPY.
    What happens if I miss a day?
    One miss resets your streak. Two misses is a warning you will not receive because there is no HR. Three consecutive misses and the contract terminates you. Your shifts for that period are forfeited to the colleagues who clocked in. You can reapply immediately and start again from zero.
    Where does the money come from?
    Trading fees, and only trading fees. Pons v2 charges 1% on every trade; 70% of that is the creator share, and 100% of the creator share is the attendance pool. There is no treasury, no team allocation and no "marketing wallet". If nobody trades, payday is 0.0000 $SPY and the pay statement will say so.
    Why is payday in $SPY and not ETH or a stablecoin?
    Because it's funnier, and because it's the thing every office worker is already being paid in, forty years late, through a 401(k). Robinhood Chain lets a token pair against tokenized US stocks and ETFs, so WAGIE pays you in the S&P 500 directly. Read Handbook §4.1 for why that particular fund is stranger than it looks.
    Is the timecard on this page real?
    It runs in your browser only, so you can learn the rules before the on-chain clock opens. It enforces the same rules the contract will. Nothing you do here is written anywhere but your own browser.
    Is there a contract address yet?
    Not yet. When there is, it appears on the Company tab, in the pinned post on X, and nowhere else. Anything claiming to be WAGIE before that is not.
    Can I get promoted?
    No.
